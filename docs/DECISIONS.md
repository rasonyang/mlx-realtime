- 2026-10-23 — ITU-T G.168 echo-path coefficients are © ITU, all rights reserved: they are kept out of git (`.gitignore`), and are regenerated from the PDF when needed.
- 2026-10-23 — G3: Smart Turn uses the fp32 v3.2 file on CPU (intra_op 2, no spinning, K=2 workers). int8's doubled false-"incomplete" rate on Chinese (17.3% vs 8.3%) costs more latency through the `max_silence_ms` fallback than fp32's extra ~30 ms per evaluation. Silero runs per stream on a single-thread executor; telephone audio is upsampled to 16 kHz, because native 8 kHz mode drifts on long calls (S7).
- 2026-10-23 — Silero's periodic state reset (pipecat policy) is off by default (`turn.vad.model_reset_interval_s: null`). S7.4 found that it adds a late-onset tail on 16 kHz audio and does not cure native 8 kHz drift. It stays available in config.
- 2026-10-23 — Audio model pinning: during the M2 real-backend validation, test passing the pinned snapshot path as the `model` to mlx_audio.server. If it works, pin audio models exactly that way; otherwise keep the repo id plus `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`, with the supervisor's pin-mismatch warning.
- 2026-10-23 — M2 was developed against faithful fake backends while M0's GPU measurements ran, so it would not disturb them. Real-backend validation (`pytest -m backend`, `make dev`) runs once M0's GPU work is done.
- 2026-10-23 — G7 (orchestrator, under the M0–M7 goal; flagged for owner review): WebRTC AEC3 via pywebrtc-audio, with livekit as a configurable fallback. It is aligned by the AICC playout model, a 60 ms reference lead, a learned 20 ms tick phase and a GCC-PHAT guard. The second line is a 2000 ms onset guard, 400 ms minimum speech while playing, and echo-turn suppression. No engine met all four G7 criteria. AEC3 is far ahead of SpeexDSP and the numpy baseline, whose residual triggers the VAD in every call. Double-talk ΔCER is still pending (S11 part 2).
- 2026-10-23 — Verbatim lines (D16) answer no turn: they skip the LLM, do not wait for a pending transcript, and make no tool call. The voice lock (GA: no voice change after the first audio) also covers per-response voices, and is enforced with `cannot_update_voice` (M3).
- 2026-10-23 — Known multi-session risk, scheduled for M6: one session's next TTS segment can queue behind other sessions on the single serialized TTS stream. If its audio has all been sent, the delta gap can reach AICC's 2 s watchdog. Mitigations to build and measure in M6: a per-session audio reserve that keeps deltas flowing while the next segment waits, and earliest-deadline-first ordering of the TTS queue.
- 2026-10-23 — G1: ASR and TTS stay on stock `mlx_audio.server`. Serialized TTS streams are a known capacity limit for concurrent sessions, addressed in M6. G5 normalization scope: phone numbers, alphanumeric/dashed IDs, e-mail addresses, cardinals of 5+ digits, and in English segments IDs, numeric dates and amounts ≥ 1000; everything else stays raw (S9). TTS defaults: `streaming_interval` 0.16; vivian for zh/mixed, aiden for en, never ryan (runaway defect); per-segment silence trimming and a `max_tokens` cap proportional to the text (S3).
- 2026-10-23 — D13 (flagged for owner review): Kokoro stays the TTS fallback as the brief names it, but only for segments without Latin letters. It garbles English inside Chinese (mixed-set error 15.9% vs 2.2%). Switching the fallback to Qwen3-TTS 4-bit is a config change awaiting owner approval (S3).
- 2026-10-23 — Environment: the Mac runs in Low Power Mode on AC power, so the GPU throttles to ~0.6× under sustained load. M0 timings are labelled capped/uncapped; the owner was asked to set Energy Mode to High Power.
- 2026-10-23 — Energy Mode on AC set to High Power by the owner (pmset powermode 2). M0 timings from B1 onward are uncapped; B2's TTS/ASR numbers were measured capped (see S3).
- 2026-10-23 — M4 additions to G7 (flagged for owner review). With no echo on the line, AEC3 suppressed a talking-over caller by up to 44 dB, so barge-in came 2.5–3.4 s late or not at all. The canceller is therefore bypassed (fed a silent reference) after 1.5 s of active reference with no confirmed echo, and re-enabled on a confirmed GCC-PHAT estimate (`aec.bypass_without_echo_ms`, null disables). Also: GCC keep range [5, 450] ms; echo-turn overlap is measured in CJK characters plus Latin words; `config.mock.yaml` runs the canceller off, because a sine mock TTS looks like echo.
- 2026-10-23 — G7 revised after double-talk ΔCER (flagged for owner review): AEC3 keeps driving VAD/Smart Turn, but its suppressor costs ASR accuracy in double talk (+11 zh / +28 en points at −15 dB). A follow-up after M5 (effort max, per owner rule) picks the ASR input path — uv-reproducible Speex, raw, or AEC3 — on barge-in-realistic double talk and implements it.
- 2026-10-23 — M5: `tool_choice` `required` and forced functions are rejected permanently (mlx_lm.server cannot force a call). Calls the session did not offer are dropped with a warning. History shaping puts each tool result right after its call; the wait line is merged into the call message by default (`tool_interlude: merge`), and unanswered calls get a placeholder result (`unanswered_calls: pending`). These defaults stand until the M0 LLM spike or the backend tests say otherwise.
- 2026-10-23 — Voices: one voice per session. vivian reads zh, en and mixed text herself; M5's per-segment switch of her English to aiden was reverted, because a male voice inside a female-voiced call is jarring and vivian's English WER is 0%. Normalizer ID detection was widened (keyed IDs from 4 digits; letter-led tokens from 3 digits), so that 5-digit cardinals do not swallow short codes.
- 2026-10-23 — M6: the per-session audio reserve (`output.reserve`) and EDF TTS scheduling are on by default. One serialized TTS stream supports about 2 concurrently speaking sessions in Low Power Mode and about 3 at full speed; beyond that, responses fail with `backend_busy`. More capacity needs a second TTS process or batched generation (owner decision), and AICC's `AICC_BOT_MAX_CALLS` should be sized to it. `queue_timeout_ms` and `first_segment_grace_ms` keep their defaults: the trade-off between failing more responses and keeping every gap under 2 s is the owner's call.
- 2026-10-23 — G2: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-4bit @38740b84, with a lenient tool-call parser in backends/llm, one gateway retry on a dropped call, temperature 0 for requests with tools, and server sampling 0.7/0.8/20. AICC's 3 s windows are at risk after cold starts and instructions changes (S2): an LLM-hardening task (effort max) comes next, before S5/S6/S10.
- 2026-10-23 — G7 final (owner): dual path. AEC3 output drives Silero/Smart Turn; a SpeexDSP linear canceller, GCC-PHAT aligned, feeds the InputBuffer and ASR. SpeexDSP's BSD C sources are vendored and built as an extension through uv, so no Homebrew dependency.
- 2026-10-23 — D13 final (owner): the TTS fallback is Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-0.6B-CustomVoice-4bit @08c72cad (same speakers as the primary, Latin segments allowed); Kokoro is dropped.
- 2026-10-23 — Capacity target (owner): the Mac is for development and testing. At most 3 concurrent calls (`max_sessions: 3`); one TTS process and one LLM/ASR process each suffice. Real-time latency has priority over throughput.
- 2026-10-23 — LLM hardening adopted. backends/llm runs `mlxrt_llm.serve`: mlx_lm.server with a lenient qwen3_coder parser, guarded to mlx-lm 0.32.2, plus opt-in tools/shared-prefix cache checkpoints, both on. `--prompt-cache-size 64` (~6 GB more resident). Gateway: one retry of a dropped call before any visible text (sampled at 0.7/0.8); tool requests at temperature 0 / top_p 1.0; the new empty-conversation cue; a pseudo-call filter for replies sent without tools; prompt-cache warming on instructions/tools updates (deferred while the LLM runs, deduplicated, one at a time). Measured: every AICC case is inside the 3 s windows at p95. The tightest is a tool-only transfer (≈ 2.2–2.6 s with ASR).
- 2026-10-23 — Request to AICC (owner to relay): keep `transfer_to_agent`'s arguments lean (a short `reason`; fill `summary`/`slots` after the transfer). The call is decode-bound at 96–176 tokens.
- 2026-10-23 — Power: `pmset` shows AC powermode 0 (Automatic) again, not 2 (High Power). One hardening run lost ~30% decode speed partway through. The owner is asked to keep High Power on AC during latency runs.
- 2026-10-23 — G7 dual path implemented: AEC3 feeds VAD/Smart Turn; vendored SpeexDSP 1.2.1 (64 ms filter, preprocessor on, 3.8 kHz pre-filter, GCC-PHAT aligned to a 20 ms target with a [0, 44] ms keep) feeds the ASR (`aec.asr_input: speex`). Double-talk ΔCER meets C2 in all 8 cells (worst +1.8 zh / +1.6 en at −15 dB); CPU ≈ 9.3 ms per audio-second per session. `processed` is renamed `aec3`. The built-in vivian voice reads English herself (one voice per session), matching config.yaml.
- 2026-10-24 — G4 (proposed, flagged for owner approval): the latency budget per stage, P95 from speech end with AICC's 500 ms silence, is VAD 550 ms, Smart Turn 100 ms (up to `max_silence_ms` when it holds a turn), ASR 400 ms, LLM first token 500 ms warm, first sentence 250 ms, TTS first chunk 150 ms alone (2 s when shared), first audio sent 200 ms. Targets, speech end → first audio on turns that end on a complete verdict: one call P50 ≤ 1.5 s / P95 ≤ 1.8 s, met (1.37–1.39 / 1.52–1.54 s); up to three calls P50 ≤ 1.6 s / P95 ≤ 2.5 s, P50 met but P95 not met at three calls (2.53–2.78 s). One call holds AICC's deadlines with margin, including a full re-prefill (~2.4 s worst case); three calls do not reliably (one first audio at 3.39 s, 7 `backend_busy` failures, 1.8 % of the three-session responses), because the single serialized TTS stream queues first segments for up to 1.9 s (`docs/LATENCY.md` §4).
- 2026-10-24 — G6: keep three model processes (LLM, ASR, TTS). The 30-minute soak through `make dev` had no hang, crash, backend error or GPU kernel message, with flat memory, and each backend recovers on its own restart. A merge would fix nothing: the capacity limit is the TTS stream itself, which a merge would not widen (real-stack validation, S6).
- 2026-10-24 — G.711 input resampling stays at soxr LQ (`g711.input_quality`). HQ is no more accurate (HQ − LQ = +0.12 MER points, 95 % CI −0.42 to +0.60, on the same 126 μ-law streams), and LQ reports the caller's speech ~30 ms sooner.
- 2026-10-24 — The audio reserve's keepalive silence is on by default (`output.reserve.keepalive_silence`; flagged for owner review): once the reserve has nothing left and more audio is to come, a 20 ms silence delta goes out every `keepalive_ms`, never before a response's first audio. With two sessions a next segment waited 2.3 s in the TTS queue and the wire went quiet for 2092 ms, past AICC's 2 s watchdog; in the three-session soak 23 responses were bridged and no gap exceeded 1.0 s. Truncation and D5 measure a segment over its speech, not this silence.
- 2026-10-24 — Leaving the echo canceller's no-echo bypass takes three consecutive valid GCC-PHAT windows that agree within 2 ms, each with a PSR ≥ 10 (flagged for owner review). The estimator's own two-window confirmation let chance peaks in a caller's double talk turn AEC3 back on over the caller (false resumes 29 → 2 in 4.5 minutes of FLEURS double talk; the SDK barge-in on the real stack went from 1503 to 539 ms); a real echo resumes one window (0.5 s) later.
- 2026-10-24 — Readiness re-warm: after the startup warmup the gateway probes each warm backend every 5 s and warms one that answers "not ready" again until it is ready; `/readyz` and `mlxrt_backend_ready` require ready and warm. A restarted `mlx_audio.server` loads a model only when a request asks for it, so a caller's turn paid for the load and the TTS fallback was never reloaded (`/readyz` 503 for good); now `/readyz` is 200 again 1.7 s (ASR), 10.4 s (TTS) and 3.0 s (LLM) after a restart.
- 2026-10-24 — Open, for the owner: TTS capacity at two and three calls. Options: a second TTS process (the provider takes one `base_url`, so a pool is a code change), mlx-audio's batched generation, shorter continuation segments (`text.segmenter.max_chars`), or a longer queue timeout for later segments (`providers.tts.queue_timeout_ms`, now that the keepalive silence feeds AICC's watchdog). AICC's `AICC_BOT_MAX_CALLS` should be sized to whatever is decided.
- 2026-10-24 — ASR context (issue #1): `session.audio.input.transcription.prompt` now biases Qwen3-ASR toward the words it names. The gateway sends it as a `context` form field; stock `mlx_audio.server` 0.6.1 accepts that field but drops it, because Qwen3-ASR's `generate` takes `system_prompt` and no `context`. The ASR backend therefore runs `mlxrt_audio.serve` (`backends/audio`), which wraps the loaded model's `generate` so `context` becomes `system_prompt` (unless a call sets one), pinned to mlx-audio 0.6.1 and self-tested at start like `mlxrt_llm.serve`; the TTS stays on stock `mlx_audio.server`. `providers.asr.context` (default unset) is the deployment default for sessions that set no prompt, because AICC's gateway profile sends none; a session prompt replaces it, and an empty prompt is no prompt. The prompt in force at commit is the turn's context, so a `session.update` applies from the next turn and never re-transcribes a committed one. At most 1024 characters: a longer prompt is `invalid_value` on `.prompt` and the session is unchanged; a longer config default fails config validation. With no context the request form is unchanged, and the warmup sends none. Measured (`spikes/asr_context`, 240 synthetic G.711/noise/onset/echo clips, 84 ASCEND human clips): a 300-character context (a sentence on the call plus the term list) raises the term hit rate from 68.7 % to 85.3 % with no non-term CER regression; concrete order-number examples in it are copied into transcripts (order-number hits 92 % → 74 %), so a context names terms, not codes; it costs about 60 ms per turn at one call and 230 ms at three (p50), a 1000-character one about 170 / 570 ms.
- 2026-10-24 — Transcription sessions (issue #2, AICC's human-call tap; `docs/PROTOCOL.md` §3.1): `session.type: "transcription"`, selected by `?intent=transcription` or by a `session.update` while the session has had no audio, item or response (after that `invalid_value` on `session.type`; the new type starts from its defaults, keeping only the input format and transcription settings). No LLM, TTS, VAD, Smart Turn, echo canceller or barge-in; `response.create`, `response.cancel` and item events draw `invalid_event` naming the event, the session stays open. `turn_detection` is `null` by default and any other value is `invalid_value` on `session.audio.input.turn_detection`; formats are the realtime sessions' (24 kHz PCM, G.711, other rates rejected on `.rate`); an unknown `transcription.model` is accepted and echoed, as in realtime sessions, because the ASR is `providers.asr.model`. One commit gives `committed`, `item.added`, `completed` (with `language` when known; no deltas); an empty or silent commit (no 20 ms frame at RMS 400; 100 at first, raised by the measurement below) is answered with an empty transcript and no ASR request, never an error, so the client need not know whether a leg went quiet. Silence costs nothing: until a voiced frame arrives the input buffer keeps its last 1 s (`transcription.idle_keep_ms`), and speech over `input_audio_buffer_max_ms` loses its oldest audio with a warning instead of ending the session (AICC commits only after speech, so a 30–90 s hold is never committed); no idle timer exists, and `max_session_s` stays 3600. Items are dropped once their transcript is out, so hours of utterances keep no memory. Turns log `session_type: transcription` and feed the existing `asr_done` stage metric.
- 2026-10-24 — Capacity target raised (owner, issue #2): `gateway.max_sessions` 4, because a human call opens two transcription sessions; they count against the limit like realtime ones, and the 5th still gets `session_limit_reached`. `providers.asr.max_in_flight` 4 (a turn per session; otherwise a transcription commit queues behind a bot turn and can hit `queue_timeout_ms`) and `providers.llm.max_in_flight` 5 (a response per session at most, plus a warm-up). The TTS stays the limit for bot calls; the AEC and Smart Turn workers (2 each) are unchanged, and two busy transcription sessions cost the bot calls nothing measurable (below).
- 2026-10-24 — Transcription measurement (issue #2, `spikes/transcription`, 24 synthesised calls, 157 minutes, G.711 tap, AICC's pump rules; `docs/PROGRESS.md`): `transcription.voiced_rms` 100 → 400. At 100 the idle noise of a moderate (RMS 80) or noisy (200) leg is "voiced", the buffer never trims, an utterance reached the ASR behind up to 58.7 s of noise (211 of 1908 behind more than 3 s), and Qwen3-ASR wrote the prompt's term list in front of it: speech CER 11.98 % at 100, 2.38 % at 400 (prompt = the term list; agent 0.53 %, customer 6.58 %). 400 lies under AICC's endpointing level (500), so every commit AICC sends is voiced, and speech lost at the start was 0 at every setting tested (`idle_keep_ms` 1000 stays). `asr.max_in_flight` 4 and `queue_timeout_ms` 1000 stay: no timeout or `queue_timeout_ms` failure in the real-time runs (commit → `completed` P50 / P95 250 / 342 ms with one session, 226 / 332 with two legs, 221 / 321 with two bots speaking beside them; 45 s of speech costs 1.2 s), and the bots' first audio does not regress (answers 638 / 1306 ms alone, 606 / 1273 ms beside two transcription sessions). The prompt helps (clean calls: CER 0.90 % with it, 1.55 % without). A non-speech commit was the defect: AICC's pump commits hold music and DTMF (above RMS 500); with a prompt Qwen3-ASR answers them with the prompt's term list (16 of 16 in the corpus), without one it runs to its token limit (30–61 s of the single ASR stream, and it keeps generating after the gateway gives up; 578 of 1924 commits failed `backend_busy`/`backend_timeout` behind it in a pass at 4× speed). A bug found: a `session.update` that changed the session type before any audio did not restart the turn detection (the previous settings were read after the switch); fixed, with tests for both directions.
- 2026-10-24 — Non-speech commits (issue #2, owner-approved; `spikes/transcription`, `docs/PROGRESS.md` "Mitigations"): two changes, both on by default. (1) `providers.asr.token_cap: {base: 16, per_s: 10}`: every ASR request carries `max_tokens = base + per_s × seconds of audio` (realtime and transcription sessions share the stream); the densest corpus utterance needs 5.1 tokens per second of speech (3.6 with the pump's silence), so the cap leaves 2.6× or more and cut no transcript; a runaway ends after 46 tokens on a 3 s tone burst (0.3 s, from 30 s) and 616 on a 60 s hold (about 4 s). `mlxrt_audio.serve` already passed `max_tokens` through (backend test added). (2) `transcription.min_speech_ms: 224`, `speech_threshold: 0.5`: a voiced commit of a transcription session is scored by the session's Silero stream (shared Silero executor, frame by frame, stopping at the first 224 ms of speech) and, below that, answered with an empty `completed` and no ASR request (`mlxrt_transcription_skipped`); a detector failure counts as speech; 0 disables. Tuned on 1908 speech commits and the 16 music and DTMF commits of the corpus: at 0.5, 224 ms skips 16 of 16 (the most speech in one is 192 ms) and loses 40 real commits (2.1 %, mostly "嗯", "行", "好的", but also a few short sentences); 0.7 with 96 ms loses 34 but sits within 32 ms of the largest tone. Result on the real stack: non-speech commits empty in 24–259 ms with and without a prompt (before: the prompt's term list, or 30–61 s of runaway), CER on the whole corpus with the prompt 1.11 % (before 23.06 % on all commits, 2.38 % on speech only; agent 0.57 %, customer 2.33 %), without the prompt 1.68 % with 0 failed commits at 4× (before 578 of 1924), bots' first audio beside two sessions carrying music and DTMF 669 / 1573 ms P50 / P95 against 698 / 1704 ms alone, commit → `completed` unchanged (251 / 347, 212 / 327, 211 / 335 ms with 1, 2 and 2 + 2 bots). The ASR backend keeps generating when the client gives up (a 2 s abandoned DTMF request made the next 3 s clip wait 28.4 s), which is why the cap, not the gateway's timeout, bounds a runaway.
