# mlx-realtime — Latency report

> **Status: first real report (2026-10-23/24).** §3 and §4 are the real stack (the conditions in
> §3.1). The mock-stack run of M6 is kept in §5 as a format example only; its numbers say nothing
> about the service.

This report answers two questions on the real stack: how long a caller waits after they stop
speaking, stage by stage; and how accurately their speech is transcribed. It also checks AICC's
deadlines on every response. It is generated by `bench/bench.py`, which talks to `/v1/realtime`
exactly as a telephone client does, and, for AICC's own call flow with tools, by
`spikes/realstack/aiccflow.py`.

## 1. How the numbers are produced

**Input.** `spikes/data/manifest.jsonl`: the 126 public M0 utterances (FLEURS zh and en, ASCEND
code-switched; `spikes/data/README.md`), 42 per set, 25 with numbers, 1.6–9.9 s each (782 s in all).
Each row has three copies; `--variant` picks one:

| Variant | Audio | Sent as |
|---|---|---|
| `tel8k` (default) | the 8 kHz G.711 stream (84 μ-law, 42 A-law), the telephone channel | decoded, resampled to 24 kHz `audio/pcm`; with `--codec pcmu`/`pcma` as G.711 (the original bytes when the law matches, the other law transcoded through 24 kHz PCM) |
| `tel16k` | the same telephone copy, back at 16 kHz | resampled to 24 kHz |
| `wide` | the 16 kHz wideband copy | resampled to 24 kHz |

Any other manifest (`audio`, `text` per row) or a directory of WAVs with a `.txt` transcript beside
each also works; a WAV may be any PCM16 rate (8 kHz included). `--sets`, `--ids` (a regular
expression), `--numbers include|exclude|only` and `--limit` select a subset.

**Replay.** One session per worker (`--concurrency N` workers in parallel, a new session every
`--turns-per-session` utterances, 10 by default), configured as AICC configures it: `server_vad`
with `silence_duration_ms` 500, `create_response: true`, transcription on (language detected),
and the instructions 你是电话客服。请用一句话简短回答。 Each utterance is sent in 20 ms
`input_audio_buffer.append` frames at real-time speed, then silence frames, without a break, until
the turn is answered: every committed item transcribed, every response done, and the answer played
out (its first delta plus its audio duration, as the gateway models the client playing it), then
300 ms. The caller listens before speaking again. (Before this report the next utterance started
300 ms after `response.done`, while the answer, sent up to 10 s ahead of real time, was still
playing in the gateway's model: every turn was double talk to the echo canceller and the barge-in
rules. `--overlap` keeps that timing.) A turn not detected after 6 s of silence is given up; 60 s
in all. The client plays nothing, so there is no echo.

**Per utterance.**

- *Client end to end*: from the moment the client sent the end of the speech to the first
  `response.output_audio.delta` of the response that answered it. The end of the speech is the
  last 20 ms frame within 35 dB of the loudest and above −50 dBFS; this energy estimate usually
  lies later than Silero's, so the client figure tends to be lower than the server's.
- *Server stages*, from `GET /debug/turns?session_id=…` (the gateway must run with
  `observability.debug_endpoints: true`): each stage named after the mark that ends it
  (ARCHITECTURE §9), and the server's end to end, `first_audio_sent − speech_end`.
- *Transcript*: `conversation.item.input_audio_transcription.completed` (concatenated when the VAD
  cut the utterance into several turns; the report counts those).
- *AICC's windows*, for every response (client arrival times): `response.created` → its first
  audio delta (AICC: ≤ 3 s), the longest gap between its deltas and the last delta →
  `response.done` (AICC's watchdog: < 2 s), and `response.created` → `response.done` for a
  response without audio (a tool-only one: ≤ 3 s). AICC's watchdog arms 3 s at
  `response.created`, re-arms 2 s at every delta and is disarmed by `response.done`.

| Stage | From → to | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| `speech_stopped` | `speech_end` → `speech_stopped` | the VAD's end-of-speech decision: `silence_duration_ms` plus detection |
| `turn_confirmed` | `speech_stopped` → `turn_confirmed` | Smart Turn's verdict (0 when silence alone ends the turn); up to `max_silence_ms` when it holds a turn it judges incomplete |
| `asr_done` | `turn_confirmed` (or `committed`) → `asr_done` | the ASR request |
| `llm_first_token` | the later of `asr_done` and `response_created` → `llm_first_token` | the LLM's time to first token |
| `first_sentence_ready` | `llm_first_token` → `first_sentence_ready` | the segmenter waiting for the first sentence |
| `tts_first_chunk` | `first_sentence_ready` → `tts_first_chunk` | the TTS queue wait and time to first chunk |
| `first_audio_sent` | `tts_first_chunk` → `first_audio_sent` | trimming the TTS's leading silence (~0.45 s of audio to synthesize), encoding and the first delta |

**Report.** Per-stage and end-to-end P50/P95 (linear interpolation), AICC's windows (P50/P95/max
and the count at or over each limit), and ASR error rates pooled over each set with M0's
`text_norm` rule (`bench/scoring.py`, as S4 scored): CER for `zh`, WER for `en`, MER (one token per
CJK character, one per Latin word) for `mix` and for all rows together. Rows with numbers are
reported apart, because references keep digits that speakers say as words. `report.json` keeps
every turn and response; `report.md` holds the tables.

The same latency numbers are on `/metrics` while the service runs: `mlxrt_stage_seconds{stage}`,
`mlxrt_e2e_seconds`, and the windowed `mlxrt_stage_p50_seconds` / `mlxrt_stage_p95_seconds` over
the last `observability.quantile_window_turns` turns.

## 2. Generating the report

```sh
MLXRT__OBSERVABILITY__DEBUG_ENDPOINTS=true make dev        # terminal 1: backends and gateway
uv run --locked python -m bench.bench \
    --url ws://127.0.0.1:8000/v1/realtime \
    --input spikes/data/manifest.jsonl --variant tel8k --codec pcmu \
    --concurrency 1 --out bench/results/real-tel8k-pcmu-c1  # terminal 2
python3 -I spikes/realstack/bench_tables.py bench/results/real-*   # the tables below
```

The runs below: `--variant wide`, and `tel8k` with `--codec pcm` and `--codec pcmu`, each at
`--concurrency` 1, 2 and 3 (`make bench BENCH_ARGS="…"` runs the same bench). The three-session
runs are the first three passes of the soak (`zsh spikes/realstack/soak.sh 1800`, PROGRESS.md).
AICC's call flow with tools: `zsh spikes/realstack/aiccflow.sh 10`. `bench/results/` and
`spikes/data/` are git-ignored; the reports of the runs below are kept in
`spikes/results/realstack_bench/`, and the soak's memory samples and the AICC flow's responses in
`spikes/results/realstack_*`.

The mock stack, no backends (the gateway runs in-process with `--serve`):

```sh
uv run --locked python -m bench.bench --serve config.mock.yaml --ids '_00[1-4]$' \
    --turns-per-session 1 --concurrency 2 --out bench/results/mock-example
```

The medians barely move; the tails are the **TTS queue**. The TTS server synthesizes one stream at
a time, so a session's first segment can wait for another session's segment to finish: up to
1.9 s (`tts_queue_ms`), which moves `tts_first_chunk`'s P95 from 80 ms to 0.6–1.4 s. A segment of
N seconds of speech holds the TTS for about N/4 s (RTF 0.25), and a one-sentence answer's second
segment is often 6–9 s of speech. Under the same load the ASR's P95 rises by 0.1–0.2 s and the
LLM's by 0.05–0.15 s.

Eight of the 1 173 responses of these nine runs failed with `backend_busy`, one at two sessions
and seven at three (1.8 % of the three-session responses): their segment waited longer than
`providers.tts.queue_timeout_ms` (2 s) past its deadline. Seven were first segments (the client
saw `error` and `response.done{failed}` 2.4–3.2 s after `response.created`); one failed after its
first audio, when its second segment timed out.

  has one verbatim line per call and no tool wait line (`aiccflow.py` does not send it), so the
  wait line's share and first audio were not measured here.
- **Replay.** A hit's first audio is the pipeline's own latency (2–6 ms); the whole line is queued

### 3.7 GPU contention (S5)

On the backends directly (`spikes/realstack/contention.py`): a ~5 s Chinese sentence on the TTS
(the gateway's request), a 6 s clip on the ASR, long answers on the LLM.

| measured | alone | while the LLM decodes 1 stream | … 3 streams | while it prefills ~16k tokens | while the TTS streams |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TTS first chunk (ms, P50 / P95) | 56 / 59 | 71 / 129 | 81 / 85 | 203 / 375 | – |
| TTS RTF (P50 / P95) | 0.25 / 0.26 | 0.33 / 0.39 | 0.38 / 0.39 | **2.08 / 2.12** | – |
| ASR (ms, P50 / P95) | 172 / 197 | 194 / 203 | 215 / 226 | – | 178 / 186 |
| LLM TTFT (ms) / decode (tok/s) | 121 / 77 | – | – | – | 131 / 68 |

Decoding slows the TTS by a third to a half (RTF 0.25 → 0.33–0.38) and the ASR by 10–25 %; the
TTS costs the LLM 12 % of its decode rate. A long prefill is another matter: while the LLM
prefilled ~16k tokens (22.8 s), the TTS ran at half real time, so any session speaking then would
starve. AICC's prompts are ~1.6k tokens (a cold prefill ≈ 1.5 s); M0's suggested
`--prefill-step-size` was not tried.

### 3.8 Transcription sessions beside bot calls (issue #2)

Real time, `spikes/transcription` (tables, corpus and commands in `docs/PROGRESS.md`): a transcription
session is the client's commit to the `completed` event, with AICC's pump endpointing (600 ms below RMS
500) on G.711 tap audio.

| sessions open | commits | commit → `completed` ms, P50 / P95 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 transcription | 58 | 250 / 342 |
| 2 transcription (the two legs of a call) | 109 | 226 / 332 |
| 2 transcription + 2 bot sessions speaking | 109 | 221 / 321 |
| same, calls with hold music and DTMF (after the speech check and the token cap): 1 / 2 / 2 + 2 bots | 56 / 116 / 116 | 251 / 347; 212 / 327; 211 / 335 |

ASR time is about 25 ms per second of audio (2 s: 117 ms, 12 s: 347 ms, 45 s: 1.2 s), alone or beside three
streaming sessions. The bot sessions do not regress with two busy transcription sessions beside them (first
audio of the answers to spoken turns P50 / P95 638 / 1306 ms alone, 606 / 1273 ms beside them; the
announce 188 / 1885 and 182 / 840 ms; `asr_done` P50 / P95 167 / 260 and 170 / 275 ms). With hold music (41 s and
69 s commits) and DTMF in the transcription legs, and the Silero speech check and the ASR token cap on, the bots'
answers are 698 / 1704 ms alone and 669 / 1573 ms beside the two sessions, `asr_done` 167 / 275 and 171 / 273 ms; the
music and DTMF commits are answered empty in 11–273 ms. Without the check and the cap a non-speech commit held the
single ASR stream for 30–61 s (`PROGRESS.md`, findings and mitigations).

add a full re-prefill after an instructions change (the LLM hardening's worst case, the first
phase change of a flow after a backend start, 1.76 s against ~0.3 s warm) and it is ~2.4 s, under
3 s. At three sessions the TTS queue alone takes up to 1.9 s of it: P95 1.9–2.2 s, maximum 3.4 s.

**Proposed targets** (speech end → first audio, server side, AICC's 500 ms silence, turns that end
on a complete verdict; Smart Turn's held turns are reported apart):

| | P50 | P95 | measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| one call | ≤ 1.5 s | ≤ 1.8 s | 1.37–1.39 / 1.52–1.54 |
| up to three calls | ≤ 1.6 s | ≤ 2.5 s | 1.42–1.47 / 1.86–2.78 |
| AICC's windows, up to three calls | – | first audio ≤ 2.0 s, tool-only ≤ 2.5 s; none over 3 s, no 2 s gap | 1.27–2.23 s; tool-only 2.78 s max; one first audio of 3.39 s, eight `backend_busy` |

**Verdict.** One call meets AICC's deadlines with margin, including a full re-prefill. Three
calls do not reliably: the one serialized TTS stream is the bottleneck (queue waits up to 1.9 s,
1.8 % of three-session responses failed, one first audio over 3 s), and a tool-only transfer
reaches 2.8 s while other calls decode. The three-call target needs more TTS capacity (a second
TTS process, or batched generation), or shorter segments to bound the wait; these are owner
decisions (PROGRESS.md, Real-stack validation, "Gates").
